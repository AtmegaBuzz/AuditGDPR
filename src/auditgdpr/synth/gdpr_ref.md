VERIFIED GDPR REFERENCE (Regulation (EU) 2016/679). Cite only what is stated here or otherwise well established.

Art. 4(1) personal data; 4(7) controller; 4(8) processor; 4(12) personal data breach (breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data).
Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation: kept no longer than necessary. Art. 5(2) accountability: the controller must be able to demonstrate compliance.
Art. 6(1) lawful bases: (a) consent, (b) contract, (c) legal obligation, (d) vital interests, (e) public task, (f) legitimate interests.
Art. 7: conditions for consent; 7(3) the data subject may withdraw consent at any time and it must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
Art. 12(1) information in concise, transparent, intelligible, easily accessible form. 12(2) controller facilitates rights. 12(3) respond to a rights request without undue delay and in any event within ONE MONTH of receipt; extendable by TWO FURTHER MONTHS where necessary taking into account complexity and number of requests; the data subject is told of any extension and the reasons within one month. 12(4) if not acting, inform the data subject within one month of the reasons and of the right to lodge a complaint and seek a judicial remedy. 12(5) information and actions are free of charge; a reasonable fee or refusal is allowed only for manifestly unfounded or excessive requests. 12(6) the controller may request additional information to confirm identity where it has reasonable doubts.
Art. 13/14: information to be provided when data is collected from the data subject / not obtained from the data subject.
Art. 15: right of access; 15(1) confirmation, a copy and supplementary information (purposes, categories, recipients, retention period, source, rights, automated decision-making); 15(3) first copy free of charge, reasonable fee for further copies; 15(4) must not adversely affect rights and freedoms of others.
Art. 17(1) right to erasure "without undue delay" where: (a) data no longer necessary for the purposes; (b) consent withdrawn and no other legal ground; (c) the subject objects under Art. 21(1) and no overriding legitimate grounds, or objects under 21(2) to direct marketing; (d) data unlawfully processed; (e) erasure required by a legal obligation; (f) data collected from a child in relation to information society services. 17(2) controllers that made data public must take reasonable steps to inform other controllers. 17(3) exceptions: (a) freedom of expression and information; (b) compliance with a legal obligation or a task in the public interest; (c) public health; (d) archiving, scientific/historical research or statistics; (e) establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.
Art. 19: notify each recipient to whom data was disclosed of rectification, erasure or restriction, unless impossible or disproportionate effort; tell the data subject about those recipients on request.
Art. 20: portability: data the subject provided, where processing is based on consent or contract AND carried out by automated means; structured, commonly used, machine-readable format; right to transmit to another controller; must not adversely affect others.
Art. 21(1) right to object to processing based on 6(1)(e)/(f); 21(2)-(3) right to object to direct marketing is absolute and processing must stop.
Art. 22(1) the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, producing legal or similarly significant effects; 22(3) where permitted, the controller must implement safeguards including the right to obtain human intervention, to express a point of view and to contest the decision.
Art. 24 controller responsibility. Art. 25 data protection by design and by default. Art. 26 joint controllers.
Art. 28: processors. 28(1) sufficient guarantees; 28(2) no engagement of another processor without prior specific or general written authorisation; under general authorisation the processor informs the controller of intended changes, giving the controller the chance to object; 28(3) a binding contract setting subject-matter, duration, nature and purpose, type of data, categories of data subjects, and stipulating that the processor: (a) acts only on documented instructions; (b) ensures confidentiality of personnel; (c) takes Art. 32 security measures; (d) respects the conditions for engaging sub-processors; (e) assists the controller with data-subject rights; (f) assists with Art. 32-36 obligations; (g) deletes or returns data at the end of services; (h) makes available information to demonstrate compliance and allows audits. 28(4) sub-processors are bound by the same obligations; the initial processor remains fully liable.
Art. 30(1) controller's record of processing activities (RoPA): purposes, categories of data subjects and data, recipients, transfers, retention periods where possible, security measures. 30(2) processor's record. 30(5) exemption for under 250 employees does not apply where processing is not occasional.
Art. 32 security of processing, including pseudonymisation and encryption, ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience, ability to restore availability, regular testing.
Art. 33(1) notify the competent supervisory authority of a personal data breach without undue delay and, where feasible, NOT LATER THAN 72 HOURS after becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons; if later than 72 hours, give reasons for the delay. 33(2) a processor notifies the controller without undue delay after becoming aware. 33(3) content: nature of the breach with categories and approximate numbers of data subjects and records; DPO contact details; likely consequences; measures taken or proposed. 33(4) information may be provided in phases. 33(5) the controller documents ALL breaches, including facts, effects and remedial action, whether or not notified.
Art. 34(1) communicate a breach to affected individuals without undue delay when it is likely to result in a HIGH risk. 34(3) not required if (a) data was unintelligible (e.g. strong encryption), (b) subsequent measures ensure the high risk is no longer likely to materialise, or (c) it would involve disproportionate effort (then public communication).
Art. 35(1) DPIA required where processing is likely to result in a high risk. 35(3) in particular: (a) systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects based on automated processing, including profiling, on which decisions with legal or similarly significant effects are based; (b) large-scale processing of special categories or criminal-offence data; (c) systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale. 35(2) seek the advice of the DPO. 35(7) minimum content: systematic description of processing and purposes; necessity and proportionality assessment; assessment of risks to rights and freedoms; measures to address risks. WP248 lists nine criteria; two or more usually indicate a DPIA is needed.
Art. 36(1) prior consultation with the supervisory authority where the DPIA indicates high risk that the controller cannot mitigate. 36(2) the authority gives written advice within eight weeks, extendable by six weeks.
Art. 37(1) a DPO is mandatory where (a) a public authority, (b) core activities require regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale, or (c) core activities are large-scale processing of special categories. Art. 38(3) the DPO receives no instructions regarding the exercise of tasks, is not dismissed or penalised for performing tasks, and reports directly to the highest management level. Art. 38(6) other tasks must not result in a conflict of interests. Art. 39(1) DPO tasks: inform and advise, monitor compliance, advise on DPIAs, cooperate with and act as contact point for the supervisory authority.
Art. 44 general principle for transfers. Art. 45 adequacy decisions (the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision applies since July 2023 to certified US organisations). Art. 46 appropriate safeguards; 46(2)(c) standard contractual clauses (SCCs) adopted by the Commission. Art. 49 derogations for specific situations (to be interpreted restrictively). After the CJEU Schrems II judgment, exporters assess the destination law (a transfer impact assessment, TIA) and add supplementary measures where needed.
Art. 56 lead supervisory authority and one-stop-shop for cross-border processing. Art. 58 supervisory powers. Art. 77 right to lodge a complaint. Art. 82 right to compensation.
Art. 83(2) factors: nature, gravity, duration; intentional or negligent character; mitigation; degree of responsibility; previous infringements; cooperation; categories of data; how the authority learned of the infringement; codes of conduct. 83(4) up to EUR 10 million or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher) for e.g. Art. 8, 11, 25-39 infringements. 83(5) up to EUR 20 million or 4% for infringements of the basic principles (Art. 5, 6, 7, 9), data-subject rights (Art. 12-22) and transfers (Art. 44-49).
