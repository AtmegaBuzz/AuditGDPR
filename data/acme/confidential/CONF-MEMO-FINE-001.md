## 1. Question Presented

What is Acme Corp Ltd's aggregate administrative fine exposure under Article 83 GDPR, given the open complaint-based inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 opened by the Irish Data Protection Commission on 2026-02-03 concerning a delayed erasure request handled through the INT-Q-ERASE queue, and the compliance gaps documented in Acme's internal risk register and audit records — including the delayed erasure request (Art. 12(3) and Art. 17), the RoPA currency gap, the training shortfall in Customer Support, the Beacon AI DPIA timing issue, and the DPO reporting-line issue under Art. 38(3)?

In particular: (a) which infringements fall under the Art. 83(5) ceiling (up to EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover) versus the Art. 83(4) ceiling (up to EUR 10 million or 2%); and (b) which aggravating and mitigating factors under Art. 83(2) — including the duration and gravity of the infringements, Acme's degree of responsibility, its cooperation with the DPC, and its remediation record — will most influence the DPC's final assessment?

## 2. Short Answer

Acme's fine exposure is tiered. The live DPC inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 (opened 2026-02-03) concerns infringement of Arts. 12(3) and 17 — data-subject rights — and therefore sits under the Art. 83(5) ceiling of up to EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover. This is Acme's most serious exposure and the only matter currently before the lead supervisory authority.

The remaining gaps fall under the Art. 83(4) ceiling of up to EUR 10 million or 2% of turnover. These are: (a) the RoPA currency gap — the Art. 30(5) exemption does not apply and the register was 40% out of date at the 2026-06 internal audit; (b) the training shortfall, with 31% of Customer Support agents missing the deadline and three DSR tickets mishandled in January 2026; and (c) the DPO reporting-line issue under Art. 38(3), which our own privileged analysis (CONF-LEGAL-2026-07) rates low-probability but high-severity.

On realistic quantum: Kwame Mensah's audit (CONF-AUD-2026-02) estimated combined RoPA and training exposure at EUR 250,000–900,000. The Art. 12(3)/17 inquiry is likely to attract the largest single element, and Art. 83(2) aggravators — the documented duration of the gaps and the CEO/CFO deferral of privacy hires over written dissent — cut against Acme.

We recommend: (a) settle inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 without formal proceedings, consistent with CONF-LEGAL-2026-03; and (b) remediate the DPO reporting line via the charter amendment sponsored by Margaret Ellison targeted for the 2027-01 board meeting.

## 3. Facts

The facts below are drawn from Acme's confidential risk register (INT-RISK-REG), internal audit records, privileged legal analyses and board records. They are stated as documented; no speculation is included.

### 3.1 The live DPC inquiry (CONF-DPC-2026-0117)

On 2026-02-03, the Irish Data Protection Commission, as Acme's lead supervisory authority, opened a complaint-based inquiry (reference CONF-DPC-2026-0117) concerning a delayed erasure request handled through the internal erasure queue INT-Q-ERASE. The delay engages Arts. 12(3) (one-month response deadline) and 17 (right to erasure), both of which fall under the Art. 83(5) fine ceiling. Priya Raman's privileged analysis of the inquiry (CONF-LEGAL-2026-03) estimates exposure up to EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover and recommends settling the matter without formal proceedings. This is the only matter currently before the DPC.

### 3.2 RoPA currency gap

Confidential risk CONF-RISK-07 records that Acme cannot rely on the Art. 30(5) small-organisation exemption: although Acme has 450 employees, its processing is not occasional. The internal audit conducted by Kwame Mensah's team as of 2026-06 found the Art. 30 record of processing activities was 40% out of date. An Art. 30 infringement sits under the Art. 83(4) ceiling (up to EUR 10 million or 2% of turnover). Kwame Mensah's audit report CONF-AUD-2026-02 estimated realistic DPC fine exposure from the combined RoPA and training gaps at EUR 250,000–900,000.

### 3.3 Training shortfall and DSR mishandling

Confidential risk CONF-RISK-04 records that the 2025 training completion rate of 96% concealed a concentrated gap: 31% of Customer Support agents in Owen Gallagher's team had not completed the DSR-handling course INT-COURSE-SUP-115 by the 2025-12-15 deadline. Three of those agents subsequently mishandled DSR tickets in January 2026. The DPO assessed this as a monitoring failure under Art. 39(1)(b), since the mishandled tickets occurred while the DPC's erasure inquiry was already open.

### 3.4 DPO reporting-line concerns

The DPO documented two Art. 38(3) independence concerns in memo CONF-MEMO-DPO-2026-04. First, the DPO's quarterly compliance reports to the CEO are routed in practice through Priya Raman, General Counsel, who twice (2025-11 and 2026-05) edited the reports to remove items she considered legally sensitive — meaning the DPO does not in fact report directly to the highest management level. Second, the DPO's 2026 performance review was drafted by the General Counsel rather than the CEO, creating a perceived penalisation risk under Art. 38(3). No remediation of the reporting line had been agreed as of 2026-09-15. Priya Raman's privileged analysis CONF-LEGAL-2026-07 (2026-08-14) rates the issue low-probability but high-severity: an adverse DPC finding would fall under Art. 83(4).

### 3.5 Board remediation decisions and dissent

Board resolution CONF-BRD-2026-06-R3 (board meeting 2026-06-18, chaired by Margaret Ellison) approved a privacy remediation budget of EUR 1.8 million for FY2027 covering RoPA tooling, DPIA automation and training platform upgrades, but cut the requested headcount from four additional privacy staff to two, rejecting the DPO's and Mara Okafor's staffing assessment. As part of the same resolution, CFO Helen Zhou argued the two approved hires could be deferred to Q2 FY2027 to protect margin. The DPO and Kwame Mensah dissented in writing, warning that the open DPC inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 made the deferral indefensible under the Art. 83(2) cooperation and mitigation factors; that dissent is recorded in the board minutes.

### 3.6 Beacon AI DPIA timing

For completeness, the register (CONF-RISK-11) records that the Beacon AI lead-scoring feature launched on 2025-10-06, two weeks before its DPIA-2025-014 mitigation sign-off was complete, breaching Acme's own INT-FORM-07 privacy-review gate; the Privacy Steering Group accepted the launch retroactively on 2025-10-28 and Lena Fischer's engineering team completed remediation on 2025-12-05.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 The statutory ceilings

Article 83(5) caps fines for infringements of the data-subject rights provisions (Arts. 12–22) at EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Article 83(4) caps fines for infringements of, among others, Arts. 25–39 — including Arts. 30, 35, 38 and 39 — at EUR 10 million or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. The classification of each infringement therefore matters as much as its substance.

EDPB Guidelines 04/2022 on the calculation of administrative fines confirm the two-step method the DPC will apply: first, determine the starting amount based on the turnover-linked ceiling attributable to the specific infringement, then adjust upward or downward using the Article 83(2) factors, taking into account the economic conditions of the undertaking. Where multiple infringements are found, the Guidelines contemplate an overall assessment rather than mechanical stacking of separate fines.

### 4.2 The erasure delay: Art. 83(5) tier

The live inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 concerns a delayed erasure request handled through INT-Q-ERASE. Article 12(3) requires a controller to respond without undue delay and in any event within one month; Article 17 requires erasure without undue delay. Both provisions sit squarely in the Art. 83(5) tier (up to EUR 20 million or 4% of turnover), as Priya Raman's analysis in CONF-LEGAL-2026-03 recognises.

Applying the Art. 83(2) factors: the delay was negligent rather than intentional — a queue-handling failure, not a deliberate refusal — which favours the lower end of the band. Against that, the inquiry is complaint-based, meaning the DPC learned of the infringement from the data subject rather than from Acme's own breach reporting, a factor EDPB Guidelines 04/2022 treat as increasing the starting amount. The duration of the delay, the number of data subjects affected and Acme's degree of responsibility will drive the quantum; a single mishandled request is at the low end of gravity, but the DPC will examine whether the failure was systemic. Notably, the three DSR tickets mishandled in January 2026 (CONF-RISK-04) occurred while this inquiry was already open, which the DPC could read as evidence that the failure was systemic rather than isolated — and as poor cooperation under Art. 83(2).

### 4.3 RoPA and training failures: Art. 83(4) tier

The Art. 30 RoPA gap (CONF-RISK-07) is an Art. 83(4) infringement (up to EUR 10 million or 2% of turnover). The Art. 30(5) exemption is unavailable: Acme has 450 employees but its processing is not occasional. The register was 40% out of date at the 2026-06 internal audit. This is a negligence-based accountability failure of significant duration; it also weakens Acme's ability to demonstrate compliance under Art. 5(2), which the DPC may treat as compounding rather than isolated.

The training shortfall (CONF-RISK-04) — 31% of Customer Support agents missing the INT-COURSE-SUP-115 deadline and three subsequent DSR mishandlings — is best characterised as an Art. 39(1)(b) monitoring failure attributable to Acme, again at the 2% tier. Taken together, Kwame Mensah's audit (CONF-AUD-2026-02) estimated realistic exposure from the combined RoPA and training gaps at EUR 250,000–900,000, which we consider a sound working range under the EDPB Guidelines 04/2022 methodology given Acme's size and the non-intentional character of both gaps.

### 4.4 The DPO independence issue: low-probability, high-severity Art. 83(4) exposure

CONF-LEGAL-2026-07 rates the Art. 38(3) reporting-line issue low-probability but high-severity. If the DPC found that the DPO is not free of instructions and does not in practice report to the highest management level — because quarterly reports are routed through the General Counsel, who twice edited them (2025-11 and 2026-05), and because the DPO's 2026 performance review was drafted by the General Counsel — the infringement would fall under Art. 83(4). The probability is low because the DPC's inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 concerns the erasure delay, not governance; but if the issue surfaced, it would be highly damaging: it suggests a deliberate governance arrangement rather than oversight, and no remediation had been agreed as of 2026-09-15. The proposed charter amendment sponsored by Margaret Ellison, targeted for the 2027-01 board meeting, is the correct mitigation and should be pursued urgently.

### 4.5 The aggravating effect of the board's hiring deferral

The most serious Art. 83(2) aggravator is the board's decision in CONF-BRD-2026-06-R3 to cut the requested privacy headcount from four to two, with CFO Helen Zhou arguing the two approved hires could be deferred to Q2 FY2027 to protect margin — while inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 was live. The DPO and Kwame Mensah dissented in writing, expressly warning that the deferral was indefensible under the Art. 83(2) cooperation and mitigation factors, and that dissent is recorded in the board minutes. The DPC, if it obtained the minutes, could treat this as evidence of a conscious decision to deprioritise remediation of the very failures under inquiry, cutting against the "degree of responsibility" and "mitigation" factors and potentially pushing the starting amount upward. We recommend the deferral be reversed before any DPC engagement on quantum.

### 4.6 Mitigating factors

Two factors work in Acme's favour. First, the board approved a EUR 1.8 million FY2027 privacy remediation budget covering RoPA tooling, DPIA automation and training platform upgrades — a concrete, funded mitigation the DPC can verify. Second, the Beacon AI DPIA timing breach (CONF-RISK-11) — launch on 2025-10-06 before DPIA-2025-014 sign-off — was fully remediated by Lena Fischer's team on 2025-12-05, within the six-week deadline the Privacy Steering Group set. Completed, documented remediation is precisely what Art. 83(2) contemplates and EDPB Guidelines 04/2022 reward. The Beacon AI matter is unlikely to attract a standalone fine given remediation and the Art. 22 safeguards now in place, but it evidences a pattern of process-gate breaches the DPC could weigh in the overall assessment.

### 4.7 Conclusion on exposure

Aggregate realistic exposure is dominated by the Art. 83(5) erasure matter; the Art. 83(4) gaps are individually smaller but cumulative, and the hiring deferral materially aggravates all of them. Settlement of CONF-DPC-2026-0117 without formal proceedings, reversal of the deferral, and completion of the DPO charter amendment are the three levers that most reduce expected fine quantum.

### 5.1 Escalation to formal proceedings (R1)

If settlement of CONF-DPC-2026-0117 fails, the DPC may open formal proceedings under Art. 58. The Art. 12(3)/17 infringement carries the Art. 83(5) ceiling (EUR 20 million or 4% of turnover), and formal proceedings increase both quantum and publicity. CONF-LEGAL-2026-03's recommendation to settle without formal proceedings should be treated as time-critical.

### 5.2 Systemic accountability characterisation (R2)

The RoPA gap — 40% out of date at the 2026-06 audit, with the Art. 30(5) exemption unavailable (CONF-RISK-07) — and the training shortfall together weaken Acme's ability to demonstrate compliance under Art. 5(2). If the DPC characterises these not as isolated Art. 83(4) infringements but as evidence of a systemic Art. 24 accountability failure, elements could be re-assessed toward the higher tier, exceeding Kwame Mensah's CONF-AUD-2026-02 range of EUR 250,000–900,000.

### 5.3 Compounding DPO-independence finding (R3)

CONF-LEGAL-2026-07 rates this low-probability but high-severity. An adverse Art. 38(3) finding — reports edited by the General Counsel (2025-11, 2026-05), the 2026 performance review drafted by the General Counsel, and no remediation agreed as of 2026-09-15 — would not merely add a standalone Art. 83(4) fine; it would aggravate every other infringement by suggesting governance interference, and would undermine the DPO's credibility as Acme's Art. 39(1) contact point with the DPC.

### 5.4 Reputational and compensation exposure (R4)

Formal proceedings would be public, with consequent customer, trust-center and sales impact in a competitive B2B SaaS market. Separately, Art. 82 gives affected data subjects a right to compensation; a systemic-failure characterisation would support collective claims exceeding any administrative fine.

### 5.5 Deferred hires as non-cooperation (R5)

The board minutes of CONF-BRD-2026-06-R3 record the CFO's deferral argument and the written dissent of the DPO and Kwame Mensah. If the DPC obtains or is alerted to these minutes, the deferral of the two approved hires while CONF-DPC-2026-0117 was live is directly admissible as a failure to mitigate and a lack of cooperation under Art. 83(2), pushing starting amounts upward across all infringements. Reversing the deferral before any engagement on quantum is the single most cost-effective risk reduction available.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Settle inquiry CONF-DPC-2026-0117 without formal proceedings.** Follow CONF-LEGAL-2026-03's recommendation to engage the DPC with a view to resolving the Art. 12(3)/17 matter by settlement, before the DPC escalates to formal proceedings under Art. 58. This is the only live matter before the lead supervisory authority and carries the Art. 83(5) ceiling (EUR 20 million or 4% of turnover).

2. **Reverse the Q2 FY2027 deferral of the two approved privacy hires.** The deferral, recorded in CONF-BRD-2026-06-R3 over the written dissent of the DPO and Kwame Mensah, is the most damaging Art. 83(2) aggravator within Acme's control. Recruit both hires immediately; the cost is trivial against the fine exposure it removes.

3. **Proceed with the DPO charter amendment.** Pursue the amendment sponsored by Margaret Ellison moving the DPO reporting line directly to the CEO, per CONF-LEGAL-2026-07, targeted for the 2027-01 board meeting. Interim measure: route the DPO's quarterly reports to Daniel Whitfield unedited.

4. **Complete RoPA remediation within the funded programme.** Use the EUR 1.8 million FY2027 budget approved in CONF-BRD-2026-06-R3 to close the 40% RoPA currency gap and the Customer Support training shortfall, and document completion dates for use as Art. 83(2) mitigation evidence.

5. **Brief the CFO and CEO on realistic quantum before settlement discussions.** Helen Zhou and Daniel Whitfield should be briefed on the CONF-AUD-2026-02 range (EUR 250,000–900,000 for the Art. 83(4) gaps) and the higher Art. 83(5) exposure for the erasure matter, so settlement authority is set on an informed basis.
