This overview is published by Acme Corp for customers and prospects conducting security and privacy due diligence. It explains how Acme Corp handles the security of personal data and how it responds to personal data incidents, including its breach notification commitments under Articles 32, 33 and 34 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Questions about anything in this overview — or reports of a suspected personal data breach at Acme — can be sent to **privacy@acme.example**, which is monitored by the office of Acme's Data Protection Officer, Tomás Reyes.

## 1. Overview and Scope

This Security and Incident Response Overview is published by Acme Corp for customers and prospects who are evaluating our platform as part of their security and privacy due diligence. It explains, in plain terms, how Acme Corp protects personal data and how we detect, manage and, where required, notify the appropriate parties about personal data incidents.

Acme Corp operates a customer-engagement and analytics platform covering CRM, email campaigns, product analytics and an AI lead-scoring feature. Acme Corp Ltd is established in Dublin, Ireland, and its lead supervisory authority is the Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC).

Acme Corp has two roles under the GDPR:

- **Controller** — for personal data relating to Acme's own customers' account data, employees, website visitors and marketing activities.
- **Processor** — for customers' end-user data stored and processed in the Beacon platform. For that data, our customers act as controllers and Acme processes personal data only on their documented instructions, as set out in our Data Processing Agreement.

This overview covers both roles and applies to the entire Acme Corp platform, including the Beacon product. Questions about its contents can be directed to privacy@acme.example.

## 2. Our Commitment to Security of Personal Data

Acme Corp is committed to protecting the personal data we process, whether we act as controller or as processor on our customers' behalf. Under Article 32 of the GDPR, we implement appropriate technical and organisational measures designed to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, taking into account the state of the art and the costs of implementation.

In practice, this commitment includes:

- **Encryption and pseudonymisation** — we use industry-standard encryption to protect personal data, including data in transit and at rest.
- **Confidentiality** — access to personal data is restricted to authorised personnel who need it to perform their duties, and those personnel are bound by confidentiality obligations.
- **Integrity and availability** — we maintain systems designed to keep personal data accurate, available and resilient against loss or unauthorised alteration.
- **Restore capability** — we maintain backups and procedures to restore access to personal data in a timely manner in the event of a physical or technical incident.
- **Regular testing** — our security measures are evaluated and tested on a regular basis to verify that they continue to function as intended.

Because Acme acts as a processor for customers' end-user data in the Beacon platform, these measures also support our contractual commitments under our Data Processing Agreement, which requires us to take the measures described in Article 32 GDPR.

Further detail on our security posture is available on request through privacy@acme.example.

## 3. How Acme Defines a Personal Data Breach

Under Article 4(12) of the GDPR, a personal data breach means a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data. Acme Corp applies this definition in full, and it covers three broad situations:

- **Confidentiality breaches** — unauthorised or accidental disclosure of, or access to, personal data, for example a message containing personal data sent to the wrong recipient.
- **Integrity breaches** — unauthorised or accidental alteration of personal data.
- **Availability breaches** — accidental or unlawful loss of, or destruction of, personal data, including loss of access caused by a technical incident.

A security incident only becomes a personal data breach where it affects personal data. Acme assesses every security incident against this definition, and the commitments in the sections that follow apply to any incident that meets it — whether Acme is acting as controller or as processor.

## 4. Notifying Supervisory Authorities

When Acme Corp Ltd, acting as a controller, becomes aware of a personal data breach that is likely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, we notify our lead supervisory authority — the Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) — without undue delay and, where feasible, no later than **72 hours** after becoming aware of the breach, in line with Article 33(1) of the GDPR.

Where notification to the DPC is not made within 72 hours, it is accompanied by the reasons for the delay, as Article 33(1) also requires. Where we cannot provide full information at once, we provide it in phases as investigation of the breach progresses.

Notifications to the DPC include the information required by Article 33(3) GDPR: the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate numbers of individuals and records concerned, our Data Protection Officer's contact details, the likely consequences of the breach, and the measures taken or proposed to address it.

Where a breach is assessed as unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, Article 33(1) permits a controller not to notify the supervisory authority. Even in those cases, Acme documents the breach internally as described in Section 6 of this overview.

Questions about Acme's supervisory authority notification practice may be sent to privacy@acme.example.

## 5. Notifying Affected Individuals

Where a personal data breach is likely to result in a **high risk** to the rights and freedoms of affected individuals, Acme Corp communicates the breach to those individuals without undue delay, as required by Article 34(1) of the GDPR.

This communication is designed to be clear and to help individuals take protective steps — for example, where a breach could expose them to identity theft, fraud or other adverse consequences. It explains, in plain language, what happened, what categories of personal data were involved, the likely consequences, and the measures Acme has taken or proposes to take, together with guidance on what the individual can do.

Where Acme acts as controller — for our own customers' account data, employees, website visitors and marketing data — we make this assessment and communicate directly with affected individuals. Where our customers act as controller for end-user data in the Beacon platform, we notify the affected customer without undue delay so that they, as controller, can communicate with their end users.

## 6. Notifying Our Customers as Controllers

For their end-user data stored and processed in the Beacon platform, our customers act as controllers and Acme Corp acts as their processor. Our Data Processing Agreement therefore commits us, in line with Article 33(2) of the GDPR, to notify the affected customer without undue delay after becoming aware of any personal data breach affecting that customer's end-user data.

When we notify a customer of a breach, we provide the information they need to meet their own obligations as controller — including the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate numbers of individuals and records concerned, and the measures Acme has taken or proposes to address it. This enables the customer to assess the risk and, where required, notify a supervisory authority or communicate with affected individuals under Articles 33(1) and 34(1) of the GDPR.

Customers with questions about a breach notification, or who wish to report a suspected breach affecting their data, can contact Acme at privacy@acme.example, which is monitored by the office of our Data Protection Officer, Tomás Reyes.

## 7. Documenting Every Breach

Acme Corp documents every personal data breach — including its facts, its effects and the remedial action taken — whether or not the breach is reported to a supervisory authority, in line with Article 33(5) of the GDPR.

This internal record is maintained for all incidents that meet the definition of a personal data breach set out in Section 3 of this overview, regardless of the risk assessment outcome. It allows Acme to demonstrate accountability for its breach handling, supports our notifications to the Irish Data Protection Commission, to affected individuals and to customers where required, and feeds into our ongoing review of the security measures described in Section 2.

Questions about Acme's breach documentation practices can be directed to privacy@acme.example.

## 8. How to Report a Suspected Breach or Contact Us

If you believe a personal data breach has occurred at Acme Corp — whether you are a customer, a prospect or another interested party — you can report it by emailing **privacy@acme.example**. That address is monitored by the office of Acme's Data Protection Officer, Tomás Reyes, and reports of suspected breaches are handled without undue delay in line with the commitments described in Sections 4 to 7 of this overview.

Customers and prospects conducting security or privacy due diligence are also welcome to send their enquiries to the same address. We aim to respond to all messages promptly.
