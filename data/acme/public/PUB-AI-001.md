This statement is published by Acme Corp for customers of the Acme Beacon platform and their end users. It explains how Beacon's AI lead-scoring feature works, what data it uses, and the safeguards and rights that apply. Questions can be sent to privacy@acme.example.

## 1. About this statement

This statement explains, in plain language, how the AI lead-scoring feature of the Acme Beacon platform works and what safeguards apply to the people whose data it processes. It is written for two audiences: Acme's customers (the organisations that use Beacon) and their end users (the individuals whose lead information is stored and scored in Beacon).

The statement covers only the Beacon AI lead-scoring feature. It does not describe the wider Beacon platform, which is explained in Acme's privacy notice.

In short: Beacon's AI lead scoring assigns a predicted engagement score to sales leads. The score is used to prioritise follow-up by human sales staff. No decision about an individual is made solely by automated means.

This statement supplements, and should be read together with, Acme's privacy notice and the Acme Trust Center, which provide further detail on Acme's data protection practices.

## 2. What Beacon AI lead scoring does

Beacon AI lead scoring is an optional feature of the Acme Beacon platform. It assigns a predicted engagement score to sales leads stored by Acme's customers in Beacon. The score is a prediction of how likely a lead is to engage with follow-up outreach.

The score has one purpose: to help human sales staff decide the order in which they follow up on leads. A lead with a higher score is surfaced earlier in the sales queue; a lead with a lower score is prioritised later. Scores are a prioritisation aid, nothing more.

No decision about an individual is made solely by automated means. The score never automatically rejects, closes, or deprioritises an account on its own. Any action that would deprioritise or close an account following a "low fit" score can be reviewed by a human first, and every such flag can be reviewed by a human before any account is deprioritised or closed. This reflects Article 22 of the UK/EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which gives individuals the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects on them.

Customers remain responsible for how their sales teams act on the scores. Acme provides the score and the safeguards around it; the follow-up itself is always carried out by people.

## 3. Data used to generate scores

The lead-scoring model is trained only on two categories of data:

- **Customer-provided CRM data**: the lead records that Acme's customers enter into Beacon themselves, such as contact details, company information, and lead status.
- **Consented engagement events**: records of interactions (for example, email opens or link clicks) collected only where the individual has consented to that collection.

No special-category data under Article 9 GDPR — such as health information, biometric data, or data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, or sexual orientation — is used as a model input.

Acme does not enrich, buy in, or combine scores with data from outside these two categories. If a customer uploads data that falls outside these categories, it is not used to train or feed the scoring model.

Customers remain responsible for ensuring that the CRM data they provide to Beacon was collected lawfully.

## 4. Human review and your rights

Every lead flagged by Beacon AI as "low fit" can be reviewed by a human before any account is deprioritised or closed. The "low fit" flag is a signal to the customer's sales team, not an outcome: a person at the customer organisation decides what happens next.

Because Beacon AI lead scoring supports — but never replaces — human judgement, no decision about an individual is made solely by automated means. Even so, Acme builds in the safeguards described in Article 22(3) GDPR for the individuals whose leads are scored:

- **Human intervention**: individuals can ask for a review of any outcome based on their score by a person at the customer organisation, rather than an automated process alone.
- **Expressing a point of view**: individuals can submit their perspective on their score or the way their lead has been handled.
- **Contesting the outcome**: individuals can challenge a "low fit" score or any resulting deprioritisation and ask that it be reconsidered.

Requests for human intervention, points of view, and contests should be sent to the customer organisation that holds the lead record, as the customer decides how its sales team acts on scores. Acme supports its customers in handling these requests. Questions about how Beacon AI lead scoring processes personal data can be sent to Acme at privacy@acme.example.

## 5. How we assess risk before launch

Before any new product or feature launches — including the Beacon AI lead-scoring feature — Acme assesses whether it is likely to result in a high risk to individuals. Where it is, Acme carries out a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) under Article 35 GDPR.

Acme's process has two stages:

- **Screening**: every AI feature is screened against the nine criteria set out in the European data protection authorities' guidelines WP248 rev.01. These criteria are the widely used indicators of processing that may pose a high risk, such as automated evaluation or profiling, and systematic processing of personal data.
- **Full DPIA**: where two or more of the nine criteria are met, Acme completes a full DPIA before the feature launches. The Beacon AI lead-scoring feature went through this process before launch.

Acme's Data Protection Officer, Tomás Reyes, is involved in every DPIA, as required by Article 35(2) GDPR.

Customers who would like a summary of the DPIA for the Beacon AI lead-scoring feature can request one by emailing privacy@acme.example. Further information about Acme's data protection practices is available on the Acme Trust Center.
